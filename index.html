I study how the adoption of new technology, including general-purpose AI, shapes competition and market structure. I am applying to PhD programmes in Economics for 2027 entry, with interests in industrial organisation and technology diffusion.

I hold an MSc in Finance from Cranfield University, where my dissertation examined the impact of policy uncertainty on the performance of private equity funds, and a B.Tech in Information Technology. Before turning to research I founded a fintech company, worked in private equity and venture capital, and annotated advanced mathematics for AI model training. My research questions grew out of independent research on how AI affects firms unevenly across sectors.

Current Research

Competition Without Competitors (working title)

Work in progress

Incumbents are usually studied as threatened by new entrants. This project asks whether general-purpose AI poses a different kind of threat: a software firm may lose ground not to a rival but to a model capable of replicating its product from a prompt. I examine how competition of this kind, without a competing firm, affects concentration and market structure, and how it depends on the pace of technology adoption across sectors.
