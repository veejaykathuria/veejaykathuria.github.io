Working Papers

Market Concentration and AI Adoption: Evidence from US Industries

Veejay Kathuria · Working paper, 2026

Do more concentrated industries adopt AI earlier, or faster? I combine the US Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey with industry concentration from the 2022 Economic Census across US industries. Concentration does not predict how fast industries adopt AI under any specification. Concentrated industries reported higher AI use in 2023, but that association is driven by information, finance and professional services rather than by concentration broadly, and by 2025 no link remains once wages and other industry characteristics are held fixed. Adoption instead tracks the type of work an industry does. The results describe correlations, not causal effects, and motivate a different question: whether the threat AI poses to incumbents comes from the technology itself acting as a substitute.

Proposed Research

Substitution from Outside the Market: General-Purpose AI and Incumbent Products

Proposed PhD research

Research question. When large language models substitute for a product, how do incumbents' prices, sales and market concentration change, and do standard measures such as the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) misread that change?

Standard concentration measures assume that competition comes from rival firms inside a market. General-purpose AI can instead let customers do the work themselves, so a market can shrink while its measured concentration barely moves. The project starts with IT services, a market highly exposed to AI, and asks whether it shrinks as clients use AI for software and IT work in-house, how much of the change comes from clients replacing the service rather than firms replacing their own workers, and what measure would capture substitution that HHI misses. It combines task-level AI usage data, such as the Anthropic Economic Index, with firm-level revenue, prices and market shares, comparing more- and less-exposed markets.
